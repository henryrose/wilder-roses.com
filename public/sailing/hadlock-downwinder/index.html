Sailing · October 2026 · Race idea

Hadlock Downwinder

Salvation at the breakwater

By Henry Rose

I've been dreaming up a downwind race. It would be most interesting for singlehanded dinghies and windsurfers, and it only works on the right day: a big winter southerly.

TL;DR

Pick a January–March weekend day with a forecast southerly over 10 knots (gusts under 25). Launch at Port Hadlock, start off the beach, and run the length of Port Townsend Bay to Boat Haven.

Why

A southerly blows straight up the bay, so the whole race is one long downwind run: a chance to plane on purpose for a few miles, then finish at home.

The race starts in the relative protection of Port Hadlock, and the wind is expected to build through the race. Racers find salvation when they get inside the Boat Haven breakwater.

When we'd run it

The date is set by the forecast. We run on a day that meets all three:

  • A weekend day (Saturday, Sunday or a federal holiday) between January and March.
  • The forecast is for a southerly of more than 10 knots between 9am and 2pm.
  • Forecast gusts don't exceed 25 knots.

How we call it: a notice goes out on Wednesday when the GFS forecast meets the parameters. We confirm on Friday morning if the HRRR forecast still meets them.

The course

  • Launch: the Port Hadlock boat ramp.
  • Start: a line between a temporary mark and a committee on the beach.
  • Finish: either a line between a boat and the race mark outside Boat Haven, or the fuel dock inside the breakwater.

Logistics to work out

  • Getting boats there: we might need to ferry boats down to Hadlock.
  • Getting dollies back: dollies need to get back to Boat Haven so boats can be hauled at the finish.

Proposed safety guidelines

  • Raccoon could be a safety boat, and we could also engage with Salish Rescue.
  • Every participant carries a radio.
  • Lock in 2–3 safety boat operators, so there's at least one safety boat for every 4 participants.

Socials

Could we get a mobile sauna at Boat Haven for the finish?

— Henry
